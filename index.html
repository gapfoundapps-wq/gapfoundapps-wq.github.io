Software for Australian trade businesses

We build tools that keep small trade businesses on the right side of Australian work health and safety law, without adding paperwork to the day.

What we do

Australian WHS regulation changes constantly, and the burden of keeping up with it falls hardest on businesses with no compliance department — the stonemason with four staff, the tiler, the concrete cutter.

Gap Found builds software that tracks the rules as they change, works out what each business actually has to hold, and shows plainly what is missing. Every legal value in our software carries the date it was last checked against the primary source and a link to that source.

FIRST PRODUCT

Airtight

Crystalline silica compliance for businesses that cut, grind, drill or polish stone, porcelain, concrete, brick or tile — stonemasons, benchtop fabricators and installers, tilers, concrete cutters, bricklayers and demolition contractors.

Airtight assembles the silica risk control plan for each task and material, logs air monitoring results against the exposure limit in force on the day the sample was taken, tracks each worker's health monitoring and training, records what must be notified to the regulator and by when, and produces an audit pack.

Currently covering New South Wales, with the 1 December 2026 workplace exposure limit change tracked as it develops.

How we build

Rules are data, not code

Legal values live as dated, cited records with a link to the primary source, not as constants buried in an app. When the law moves, the software moves with it.

A person checks the law

Changes are detected automatically and confirmed by a human before they reach a customer. We will not silently rewrite a limit a business is judged against.

Honest about scope

Our software is for record keeping and compliance tracking. It is not WHS advice, legal advice or medical advice, and we say so in the product.

Sensitive data stays minimal

Where we must hold health information, we hold the least that proves the duty was met — never clinical content.
