In short

Airtight does not collect your records. Everything you enter — your business details, your workers, your air monitoring results, and the health monitoring dates the law requires you to keep — stays on your phone, encrypted. It is never sent to us. We hold no copy of it, and we could not read it if we did.

There is no account, no sign-in and no password, because there is nothing to sign in to. Your phone's own lock screen is what keeps the records private.

We do not sell data, do not share it, and do not use it for advertising. There are no advertising or analytics trackers in the app.

The other side of that. Because nobody else holds a copy, a lost or reset phone with no backup means the records are gone — and the duty to keep air monitoring and health monitoring records for 30 years stays with your business. Airtight has a Backup screen for exactly this reason, and it warns you when you have not used it.

How it is protected

On your phone: your records are held as one file encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is stored in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore, set so that it does not sync to iCloud and does not travel in a device backup. The app never writes your records anywhere else.

Your backups: a backup file is encrypted with a passphrase you choose, not with anything stored on the phone — that is what lets you open it on a replacement device. We never see the passphrase and cannot recover it for you.

The law the app applies: the rule file is signed, and your device checks the signature before trusting it. A file altered along the way is thrown away and the app keeps the rules it already had. This is what stops the exposure limit being tampered with in transit.

No keys in the app: the only keys in the app are public ones — RevenueCat's client key, which is designed to ship in an app, and the key used to check that rule signature.

Being straight about the limits: because there is no server, the check that you hold a subscription happens on your phone rather than somewhere you cannot alter. That is genuinely weaker than a server-side check, and the app says so on its Subscription screen. It is the same trade that keeps your records private: with nothing of yours on our side, there is nothing on our side to arbitrate either.

Your rights

Access and export

Everything is already in your hands. The Backup screen exports the lot as an encrypted file you keep; the Audit pack screen produces the readable version to show an inspector.

Correction

Every record is editable in the app.

Deletion

We cannot delete your records, because we do not have them. You delete them yourself: the Backup screen has a "Delete everything on this phone" control that erases the encrypted file, its spare copy and the key that opens them. Removing the app does the same thing.

That deletion is final — there is no copy anywhere for us to restore from. Air monitoring and health monitoring records must be kept for 30 years and training records for 5 years after a worker leaves, and that duty stays with your business, so take a backup before you delete anything.

Workers

A worker may ask their employer for their own records. A health monitoring report must not be disclosed to anyone else without the worker's written consent. Airtight never holds the report itself, so it cannot disclose one.

Complaints

Email us first. If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au.

Children

Airtight is a workplace tool and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone at all, at any age — the records you enter about your workers, whatever their age, stay on your device.

Changes

If this policy changes materially we will say so in the app before the change takes effect. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
